The hardest part of this assignment was parsing dates in parse_pay_date. I understood why the dates all had to be the same type, but I struggled with how to make every date match and which format codes to use, like "%m/%d/%Y" for "8/14/2026" and "%Y-%m-%d" for "2026-09-25" and when certain %letters were used and when they were not. I learned that errors="coerce" turns anything unreadable into NaT, so I could parse the column twice and fill the gaps with fillna. I also learned what grain and lineage mean and how they fit together. Lineage means row-level steps like add_clean_columns return a wider copy and never change the raw columns. Grain means what one row stands for, so a group by or pivot_table starts a new table at a new grain while the history stays untouched. I struggled with pivot_table, both in deciding what goes in index, columns and values and in knowing when to use fill_value. I also kept forgetting pieces of named aggregation, like the lambda for overtime_weeks and the reset_index() that moves the group keys out of the index.
